- Make the round count part of the noise floor `hevc_decode_profile --pair` reports, and refuse a floor too few rounds can support (issue #438). The floor is the widest the null control misses 1.0000x anywhere in its interquartile band, which is the right definition and was reported with nothing to say how many rounds underwrote it. That matters because the number moves in the flattering direction exactly where it is least trustworthy: on one i9-10850K, 5 rounds read **3.36%**, 21 rounds read 4.43% and 51 rounds read 6.37%. The 5-round draw had not found a quieter host — an interquartile band over five readings is three of them — but 3.36% is the figure a reader would take for the tightest of the three, and tightening a bound with an artefact is the class of error this instrument was built (in #431) to prevent.

  **Every ratio and every floor now names the rounds behind it**, and below `hevc_decode_profile::paired::MIN_FLOOR_ROUNDS` no floor is printed at all, the way the arms already refuse a ratio with no control behind it. The minimum is the fewest rounds at which two readings fall beyond *each* end of the band — 8, derived rather than chosen. Below it the band's end is the run's own second-most-extreme reading, so the band is the whole range with one reading shaved off each side and its width is set by where a single round landed; a run there now reports the effect as *unbounded* rather than as small, since an unmeasured floor bounds nothing.

  **Nothing about the statistic above the minimum changed.** The floor is still the widest miss of 1.0000x anywhere in the control's band, the cross-arm digest assertion is untouched, the default `--rounds 12` clears the minimum, and every draw recorded in `benches/README.md` stands as taken. What moved is where the rule lives: the band and the floor are now `hevc::engine::profile::paired` with unit tests that derive the minimum, pin the unchanged band and floor definitions, and pin the refusal, instead of a private helper in the example that only ran when someone ran the profile. `benches/README.md`'s `#434` section, which documented this trap in prose, now points at what the instrument does about it.

- Answer the plane-representation half of #378 that #404 left open, and close it on a measured null result (issue #427). #404 landed `simd::filter_taps_narrow` at 1.89x against `filter_taps` and found only one of the four §8.5.3.3.3 phase cases keeping that advantage through `interp_block`. The kernel was the same in all of them; the caller was not. The vertical-only phase reaches its taps through `RefPlane::gather`, which materializes a buffer either way, so writing it as `i16` is free; the horizontal-only and two-dimensional phases reach theirs through `RefPlane::row_window`, which **borrows the plane outright with no copy** when the window lies inside it, so narrowing there had to add a materializing pass that cost about what the wider lanes saved. The fix #378 named first was upstream of the kernel: if an eight-bit plane carried its samples as `i16`, `row_window` would borrow narrow and the pass would not exist.

  **The premise holds and the arithmetic does not.** `RefPlane` now takes an optional `i16` mirror of the plane it wraps and `row_window_narrow` borrows out of it exactly as `row_window` borrows out of the `i32` samples, so `measure_narrow_vs_wide_block` can time both representations against the same `i32` baseline in one interleaved process, all three arms asserted equal sample-for-sample first. On an Intel Core i9-10850K with AVX2 the horizontal-only phase moves from **0.90x and 0.93x to 1.18x and 1.27x** at 32x32 and 64x64 — the two sizes carrying 93% of a decode's interpolated luma samples — with no kernel touched, and the two-dimensional phase from 0.93x/0.96x to 1.09x/1.12x, less because only its horizontal pass can narrow at all. But the mirror has to be *written*, once per picture, and every decoded picture is stored as a short-term reference, so the accounting is one write against one frame of interpolation. The new `measure_narrow_mirror_amortization` takes both halves over a 1080p luma plane at the measured `INTER_PU_MIX` size mix across all sixteen Table 8-8 phase combinations: read as a best-of-many it flipped sign between invocations (0.95x, then 1.04x), which is #426's finding about this host arriving again, so the instrument is #426's — five arms paired within each round, rotating which runs first, median of the per-round ratios with an interquartile band, and a null control whose true answer is exactly 1.0000x. Over four invocations of thirty paired rounds the control reads **1.0000x to 1.0599x**, the arm that writes the mirror into a buffer that already exists reads **0.91x to 0.95x** and the one that allocates it fresh **0.79x to 0.87x** — both below the control in every invocation — while the arm that is handed a mirror nobody paid for reads only **1.04x to 1.11x**. That last row is the ceiling the other two are buying and cannot reach: even free, the mirror is worth 4% to 11% of a frame's interpolation, and writing it costs more than that. That is before the chroma planes, which add half a luma plane's worth of writing for a 4-tap filter on quarter-sized blocks, and before the 50% the second representation adds to every eight-bit picture in the decoded-picture buffer. The low ceiling is what the per-phase table predicts rather than a contradiction of it: uniformly over Table 8-8 only three of sixteen phase combinations are horizontal-only, three are vertical-only and already narrowed, nine are two-dimensional and gain about a tenth, and one does not filter, which weights out to under a tenth of the stage.

  **So the decoder keeps its `i32` planes and `narrows` keeps #404's routing.** `RefPlane::with_narrow` is `#[cfg(test)]` apparatus — the arrangement `measure_2d_ring_vs_flat` already uses for the ring #309 proposed and #420 uses for its five rect kernels — so the table stays reproducible after the decision it justifies, and `narrows` now names the condition that separates the phases (whether the window arrives narrow without a pass) rather than naming the phases, so what was measured is what the code says. `benches/README.md` records both tables, the amortization, and the one remaining way to get the borrow: making `i16` the plane's *sole* representation for eight-bit content, which removes the pass and the memory together but requires `Picture` to become an enum over sample width that every §8 consumer of `Picture::plane()` resolves — the thing #427 asked to do without. No decoded sample moves on any backend or at any bit depth, and no dispatch site changes. `the_eight_bit_block_path_matches_the_per_sample_equations` now runs **both** plane representations against the normative per-sample §8.5.3.3.3.2 / §8.5.3.3.3.3 equations, which is the guard that matters because at eight bits `every_backend_matches_scalar_luma_block` would have the narrow formulation on both of its sides, and `a_mirrored_reference_plane_decodes_the_same_samples` holds the mirrored arm against the wide one over every block shape, origin, phase and backend the host offers.
- Measure §8.5.3.3's 16-bit interpolation path on x86_64, so `inter_pred::narrows` stops routing `Avx2` and `Sse41` down it on NEON-only evidence (issue #435). One of the four conditions that decision turns on is "a vector backend", spelled `isa != Isa::Scalar`, and every measurement behind it in `benches/README.md` was taken on an Apple M1 — while `filter_taps_narrow_avx2` and `filter_taps_narrow_sse41` are real kernels that the routing has been sending work to. The gap mattered because the argument has two halves that do not transfer equally: the *who pays to narrow the source* half is about `RefPlane::gather` versus `RefPlane::row_window` and is instruction-set-independent, but the *lane-count* half is not, since AVX2's `i32` kernel already issues eight lanes per multiply where NEON's issues four. #431 could not settle it from the whole-decode side, which bounded the effect at ±4% and indistinguishable from zero.

  **Both isolated measurements now run on every vector backend the host offers, and both tables carry x86_64 columns.** `measure_narrow_vs_wide_block` swept only `detected_isa()`, so on an x86 host it could only ever produce the AVX2 column; it now walks each available backend, interleaving the backends with each other as well as the two arms. On an **Intel Core i9-10850K** the kernel sweep reaches about **2.1x** on both `sse4.1` and `avx2` at a long row, above NEON's 1.89x, and the block sweep reads the vertical-only phase — the one the routing selects — at **1.12-1.31x (`sse4.1`)** and **1.05-1.12x (`avx2`)** from 16x16 to 64x64, above parity at every width and above both other phases on both backends. **The condition therefore stands as `isa != Isa::Scalar` rather than being narrowed**, and the `narrows` rustdoc now cites a figure from each of the three backends it admits instead of NEON's alone.

  **The lane-count prediction is visible in the result, and so is the instrument's own resolution.** The win is smallest on the widest backend — 1.43x `neon`, 1.29-1.31x `sse4.1`, 1.10-1.12x `avx2` at 64x64 — which is what doubling sixteen lanes rather than eight should look like. The `scalar` control is sharper on this host than on the M1: the narrow arm is a loss at *every* row length (0.56x to 0.85x) and worse the longer the row, which is the second condition demonstrated rather than argued. Unlike the whole-decode pairing on this same machine, three consecutive invocations of the block sweep agree to within **±0.02x** on every cell against effects of 10-30%, so this instrument resolves what that one could not.

  **One gap is recorded rather than closed.** At a block width of exactly eight the vertical-only phase reads 1.00-1.02x (`sse4.1`) and 0.95-0.97x (`avx2`) against NEON's 1.37x, so the separate `w >= 8` condition is right on NEON and about a wash on x86_64. Tightening it means making the threshold instruction-set-dependent, which is a different change from the one this issue asked for and is filed as #440. No decoded sample, kernel or dispatch decision changes: the bit-exactness guards `every_backend_matches_scalar_filter_taps_narrow`, `every_backend_matches_scalar_luma_block` and `the_eight_bit_block_path_matches_the_per_sample_equations` are untouched and passing.

- Give the browser build a `SeekPreviewSource`, so `ARCHITECTURE.md` section 3.2's seek requirement is met on `wasm32` and not only on native (issue #432). #410 landed the preview tier and #416 stated the requirement it satisfies, but `previews` was gated `#[cfg(not(target_arch = "wasm32"))]` because its pass owns a thread, so nothing implemented the trait in a browser: a `wasm32` `ExactFrameReader::seek` could only ever return `Seek::Exact` from the presentation cache or `Seek::Pending`, and `examples/web_canvas` still walked forwards from a random-access point to reach a scrubbed position - seconds on the bundled sample, whose 768 frames offer exactly one place a decode can start, against the 50 ms the requirement states.

  **What was native-only was never the tier, only its driver.** `previews` is portable now, and what stayed behind the `cfg` is the loop alone. `PreviewOptions` and the stride it derives, the picture store with its nearest-with-fallback lookup, and the pass cursor that says which frame the next preview is of are shared rather than reimplemented: a new `PreviewStore` is the handle a seek reads and the `SeekPreviewSource` both drivers fill, and a new `PreviewPass` is the cursor both drive. `PreviewIndex` keeps its thread, its behaviour, its API and its tests - it is the same pass, now written as `while let Some(frame) = pass.next_frame()` over the shared cursor.

  **The browser's driver is an idle callback, because that is the thread it has.** `web_previews::WebPreviewIndex` owns a `WebCodecs` decode session of its own - for the reason the native pass owns a decoder of its own, that the pass walks forwards once and a scrub jumps - and `step()` decodes exactly one preview and returns to the event loop. A frame that will not decode leaves its position empty and the pass carries on; an aborted step is the one failure that does not advance it, so the next call asks for the same position again rather than leaving a hole a later lookup would fall through. Through the wasm bindings this is `VideoStream.previews(options)`, `PreviewIndex.step(signal)`, and a synchronous `PreviewIndex.nearest(frame)` that decodes nothing, plus `PreviewOptions`, `Preview` and a `seekLatencyBudgetMs()` export so a browser caller budgets against the same constant the library's tests hold it to.

  **`examples/web_canvas` scrubs from it.** Every pointer sample draws the nearest preview at once and the walk goes after the exact frame underneath it, instead of the drag waiting on a walk through the group of pictures; the page advances the pass one preview per `requestIdleCallback`, and an overlay reports what each seek cost against the budget. `shouldDrawPreview` keeps the one new decision - a preview stands in for a picture that is not there yet, and is never drawn over the exact frame already on the canvas - in `scrub.js` with the walk's other pure rules, where `scrub.test.js` asserts it.

- Give the HEVC encoder's per-CTB SAO search the coded rate model, and settle what pricing SAO correctly then exposes at the slice-level acceptance rule (issue #400). #384 landed `coding_bins` - an exact model of what `code_sao` writes for a cell, the §7.3.8.3 merge flags plus the `sao( )` structure only when neither merge fires - as a *measurement*, and left the search pricing band syntax through a fitted `BAND_SYNTAX_CHARGE_NUM`/`_DEN` of 2.5x, re-derived there as the 2.47x of merge a band component forfeits. The search is now scored on `coding_bins` itself, and the two merges are candidates of it in their own right: `estimate_sao` walks the grid in the raster order `code_sao` writes it in, so the left and above cells are decided by the time this one is, and a CTB that can take a nearly-as-good neighbour's parameters for one flag takes them. `BAND_SYNTAX_CHARGE_NUM`, `BAND_SYNTAX_CHARGE_DEN` and the whole `SaoLambda` pair are gone with it - there is one multiplier again, because what the second one stood in for was the merge and the search counts the merge now. That is strictly better than the constant rather than merely equivalent to it: per QP the ratio the constant averaged ran 0.50x to 4.00x, since how much merge a band component destroys is a property of how much its neighbours agreed with each other, which is content and not rate.

  Scoring candidates the writer's way means the statistics have to outlive the candidate that generated them, because a merge takes parameters chosen for a *different* CTB's error - any class, any band position, any offsets. `PlaneStats` gathers a CTB's whole §8.7.3.2 picture up front, the per-category sums and counts of all four edge classes and all 32 bands, and `PlaneStats::gain` says what an arbitrary component buys here whether or not this CTB's own search would have proposed it. The per-component searches keep scoring marginally over leaving that component off, where the alternative really is this CTB coding its own structure; the cell-level decision between "off", the two merges and the standalone structure is taken on absolute `coding_bins`, with ties broken towards the cell that codes the least.

  What blocked this in #384 was that pricing SAO correctly makes it *cheaper*, and the slice-level rule then accepted grids at QPs where it used to decline - two of them landing under the writer's own SAO-off curve at CTBs carrying no band component at all, which no band-syntax charge at any value can suppress. That was read as `SAO_ACCEPTANCE_RESOLUTION_NUM` again, a rule deciding on margins one to two orders of magnitude finer than the 50.5% the acceptance model is measured wrong by. **It is not.** A two-point model read *inside* the interval its points measure is a measurement; read outside it, it is a prediction, and that is where the failures were being decided. SAO usually spends a fraction of one quantizer step, so the coded point and the probe's rung bracket the rate and the single-chord form is exact - but not always: on smooth 128x96 at QP 21 a grid worth 0.525 dB costs 344 bits against a step of 216, which puts the rate judged 1.57 steps out, and the log-log line read that far past its own anchor on a curve that is steeper further out predicted 389 of squared error where the writer's own ladder removes 957. The rule accepted that grid by a factor of 2.4 and it landed 0.015 dB *under* the curve. `bracketed_reachable_sse` keeps the read inside the interval instead, walking further rungs of the picture's own ladder towards finer quantizers until one codes at least the rate being judged and interpolating between the last two; `sao_threshold_sse` falls back to the single-chord form when the caller will not pay for another rung, and QP 0 keeps it because there is no finer quantizer to walk to. **No tolerance is widened anywhere**, which #344 established cannot work: a margin wide enough to refuse the failures refuses the 0.100 dB win beside them.

  The search's own price is read off the same curve rather than from the closed form. `lambda_q8` is the right instrument for choosing between two codings of one CTB at one QP, where the picture cancels out of the comparison, and that is what choosing an edge class or a band position is - but it is the wrong one for a merge, which is not a choice between two codings of a CTB at all: taking a neighbour's parameters gives up distortion to spend fewer bits on the *slice*, the same trade the acceptance rule makes and the one #287 measured the closed form wrong for by 0.4x to 2.5x. `sao_search_lambda_q8` is the curve's slope at the coded point - the tangent `reachable_sse` leaves at zero SAO rate - which is the quantity for pricing one candidate and needs no rate to be read at. Reading the interpolated *average* instead would need the search's own answer as its input and make the price chase the grid it is meant to judge: measured, a picture whose structure search finds nothing is read at the rate of an all-off grid, 13 bins over 12 CTBs, where the average clamps to the trust band's fine end, the search buys SAO at a quarter of the closed form and the slice lands 0.015 dB under the curve. Because the tangent is a *local* slope, a grid that spends more than the step the probe measured has been bought outside the stretch of curve it describes, so `sao_spent_lambda_q8` re-reads what the rate actually spent is worth per bit and the search runs again whenever that is dearer than what it charged. Only dearer - a re-read that would let the search buy *more* is the same extrapolation being trusted further out, which is what put a point under the curve to begin with. Every price here is clamped to `SAO_LAMBDA_BAND` and falls back to the closed form where the two points describe no curve, exactly as `calibrated_sao_lambda_q8` does.

  `every_accepted_sao_point_sits_on_the_writers_own_curve` holds over the QP 12-51 sweep on both test pictures at both sizes, with no tolerance widened and no assertion relaxed, and so do `the_slice_level_threshold_is_monotone_in_the_grid_it_judges`, `the_acceptance_model_cannot_resolve_the_margins_it_decides_on` and `the_coded_rate_model_agrees_with_what_the_writer_spends`. #274's four operating points survive: `the_sao_pass_is_worth_taking_where_the_writer_takes_it` still clears its floors at smooth 64x48 and 128x96 QP 12 and noise 64x48 and 128x96 QP 37. `the_band_settles_the_decisions_that_fall_outside_it` replaces `the_slice_level_test_only_probes_when_the_band_leaves_it_open`, because the probe is no longer lazy - the search needs the two curve points before there is a grid to judge - so what is asserted is the property that made the laziness safe rather than the laziness: a gain clearing the band's coarse end is kept and one missing its fine end is declined whatever the curve says, both checked against a curve that would decide the other way. `the_band_syntax_charge_is_the_merge_a_band_component_forfeits` and `a_band_component_forfeits_a_merge_that_no_bin_it_codes_pays_for` are removed with the constant they measured; `sao_sweep` now reports merges per grid alongside the band components. Addresses #384 and #396, and closes out the acceptance-rule limitation #344 and #345 describe from the other side.

- Answer what §8.5.3.3's 16-bit interpolation accumulation is worth to a whole decode, by fixing the instrument that could not resolve it rather than by looking for a quieter host (issue #426). #404 landed the narrow path and left one of its own acceptance criteria unmet on the record: paired against `main` through `hevc_decode_profile`, the `neon` arm read 1.10x in one round set and 0.83x in another, while the `scalar` arm — where `narrows` is false, so both binaries provably execute identical code and the true answer is exactly 1.00x — read 1.06x and 1.07x in those same sets. A ±7% miss on a known-1.00x control cannot underwrite a low-single-digit measurement, so the `hevc_inter_pred` and `inter_pred_filter` rows were left standing on an unbounded null.

  **The confound was the instrument, not only the host.** That pairing ran two *builds* in two *processes*, which is the arrangement `measure_narrow_vs_wide_block` and `measure_2d_ring_vs_flat` both already refuse in as many words. `zvidlib::hevc_narrow_interp` is a process-level override of the `narrows` decision, and `hevc_decode_profile --pair` uses it to run both arms inside one binary, over the same decoder and the same frames, with a third arm that runs the wide arm's code again — a null control with a known answer of 1.0000x, measured in the same rounds by the same instrument, so no reading is reported without what the instrument could resolve when it was taken. All three arms are asserted to decode bit-identically, an FNV digest over every plane of every frame, before anything is timed.

Sample-weighting that sweep predicts 1.07x for the real mix and the rebuilt group measures 1.09x, against the old grid's 1.25x on the same host on the same day; neither uni-prediction nor chroma moves it, so block size accounts for the whole difference. With both accounted for, the isolated group at 1.09x and the in-decode kernel arm at 1.07x agree: the two measurements were consistent and the expectation was wrong.

- Make the native AV1 encoder's serial range coder faster, which is the first change to move the lossless encode end to end. With the §8.3.2 coefficient contexts vectorized, a `sample` profile put 64% of `FrameEncoder::encode` inside `SymbolEncoder::encode_symbol` - and that is serial by construction in the same way the HEVC §9.3.5 CABAC encoder is, so it took the two serial changes the HEVC side had already measured wins from rather than another SIMD dispatch family. First, the bit sink: `src/av1_encoder/symbol.rs` followed `od_ec` in buffering every output byte as a `u16` so a pending carry could live in bit 8, then resolving all of them in a second reverse pass over the whole stream at `finish`. It now normalizes each byte as it arrives - a carry can only sweep the trailing run of `0xFF` bytes, which the encoder tracks, so the sweep is amortised constant time - which halves the sink and drops the pass entirely. Second, the literal-bit runs: coefficient signs, the `eob` extra bits and the exp-Golomb tails were each coded a bin at a time through the generic CDF path. `encode_literal` is now a specialization of the interval update for the one CDF `read_bool` ever uses, where both branches collapse onto the same split point, so a run keeps `low`, `rng` and `cnt` in registers across all of its bits instead of reloading them per bit; and the call sites hand it whole runs, the Golomb tail as a single field because `len - 1` zeros followed by `x` in `len` bits is just `x` written in `2 * len - 1` bits.

  Best of interleaved rounds per arm on an Apple Silicon host, `--release`, five rounds at 640x352 and seven at 1920x1080: `av1_encode_stage_symbol` 2.20 ms to 1.77 ms and 21.4 ms to 16.7 ms, `av1_encode_stage_tile` 36.5 ms to 33.0 ms and 400 ms to 310 ms, `av1_encode_frame_q0` 38.4 ms to 34.9 ms and 442 ms to 318 ms - 1.24x to 1.39x depending on the group and the size, and the whole-frame groups moving with the tile group for the first time. The encoded bitstream is byte-identical: every benchmark group's `bench_across_isas` guard reports the output byte count it reported before, and the encoder's `one_frame_encodes_identically_under_every_instruction_set` and `a_fixed_frame_encodes_to_the_same_bytes_on_every_host` digests are unchanged. The unrolled literal path is asserted byte-identical against the bit-at-a-time reference engine at every run length from 1 to 24 - exhaustively over every value up to 10 bits, sampled with the extremes above that - and again over a 50,000-event stream that interleaves runs with CDF-coded symbols, so the runs are entered at arbitrary coder state rather than only from a fresh encoder. The eager carry sink is asserted against a transcription of `od_ec_enc_done`'s reverse pass over adversarial digit sequences, including a carry sweeping 64 `0xFF` bytes and a carry off the front of the stream that both engines discard. `benches/README.md`'s "Why the tile group barely moves" gains a "What the serial work bought" section, so its 64% figure now reads as the state before this rather than the state now.

- Vectorize the HEVC encoder's lossy reconstruction path. The §8.6.6 add-and-clip of the encode-side reconstruction was only vectorized for the exact-residual (PCM) writer; the quantized residual writer - the path a real encode takes - still wrote back `Clip1(prediction + coded)` one sample at a time through `Picture::set_sample`. `src/hevc/engine/encoder/recon_simd.rs` gains an i32 add-and-clip kernel with SSE4.1, AVX2 and NEON backends dispatching through the existing `hevc_recon` site, and the quantized writer now reconstructs a whole transform-block row per call. The kernels stay in 32-bit lanes because a dequantized residual does not fit the 16-bit lanes the exact-residual kernel uses, so the result is bit-identical to the scalar reference on every instruction set: asserted directly against each backend over every run length that exercises a partial vector and a scalar tail, and end to end by a reconstruction that must come out byte-identical on every instruction set at every QP the round-trip tests cover. `benches/hevc_encode.rs` gains a `*_reconstruct_quantized` arm so the path is measurable, since the existing `*_reconstruct` arm only exercises the exact-residual path. That arm also caught a latent panic in the same writer: §7.3.8.8 transform blocks bottom out at 4x4, but the 4:2:0 chroma half of an asymmetric 16x4 or 16x12 partition measures 8x2 or 8x6 and is not a whole number of them, so the residual loop indexed past the end of the chroma plane on any picture whose mode search picked one. The cause was modelling the transform tree on the prediction partition; §7.3.8.8 hangs it off the coding unit instead, which is why a 16x16 coding unit keeps an 8x8 chroma transform block however its prediction is split. The residual round trip and the §8.6.6 write-back are therefore driven from the coding unit rather than from each partition — one 16x16 luma and two 8x8 chroma transform blocks per CU, tiling it exactly, so no block is ever clipped or zero-padded and none can reach past the end of a plane. The prediction geometry is still each partition's own, gathered into a coding-unit-sized buffer that the residual coding reads whole; a picture is now asserted to reconstruct identically under every partitioning that predicts the same samples, which is the property the per-partition tree did not have.

- Vectorize the native AV1 encoder's §8.3.2 `coeff_base` / `coeff_br` context derivation, the data-parallel half of the coefficient coding loop that `benches/av1_encode.rs` identified as the lossless encoder's remaining hot spot. `src/av1_simd/coeff.rs` is a new SSE4.1/AVX2/NEON dispatch family, registered as the `av1_coeff_ctx` site of `simd::active_by_site()` so `simd::set_override` pins it like every other kernel. It derives a whole transform block's contexts in one pass ahead of the serial symbol loop rather than one position at a time inside it, which is legal because both offset tables reach only down and to the right while the loop walks the up-right diagonal scan backwards - so every neighbour a position consults is already final, or zero past the end-of-block. The levels live in a zero-padded plane whose border is written once per block size, turning each neighbour into an unaligned load with no per-lane bounds branch. Every backend is a lane-by-lane transliteration of the scalar reference, so the encoded bitstream is byte-identical under every instruction set: asserted by the existing `bench_across_isas` guard, by unit tests over every width from 1 to 18 and at 32 and 64 - covering the partial trailing vector at both lane counts - and by a test that replays the incremental derivation the coding loop used to do, for every end-of-block a block can have.

  The measured result is that this was worth less than the issue expected, and the benchmarks now say so rather than leaving it to be re-derived. A new `av1_encode_stage_coeff_ctx` group measures the pass on its own and shows the kernel plainly - 3.05 ms scalar against 1.69 ms NEON at 640x352 on an Apple Silicon host, best of interleaved rounds, 1.8x - but `av1_encode_stage_tile` and `av1_encode_frame_q0` stay within run-to-run noise of their `main` numbers, because the pass is only about 9% of a tile encode. A `sample` profile says where the other 91% is: 9,164 of 14,411 samples inside `FrameEncoder::encode` are in `SymbolEncoder::encode_symbol`. What remains of coefficient coding once the contexts are vectorized is the range coder, which is serial by construction in the same way the HEVC CABAC encoder is, so the remaining AV1 lossless headroom is a serial question - a wider bit sink, unrolled literal-bit runs - and not another dispatch family. `benches/README.md` records that under "Why the tile group barely moves".

- Vectorize the two scalar stages that dominate the pure-Rust HEVC encoder's reconstruction pass, and register them as a SIMD dispatch site of their own. `benches/hevc_encode.rs`'s `..._reconstruct` group barely separated across instruction sets because the in-loop filter kernels it calls -- the decoder's, already vectorized -- are a minority of its cost: the §8.6.6 reconstruction loop in `src/hevc/engine/encoder/recon.rs` formed the prediction, differenced it against the source and clipped one sample at a time over every plane of every partition, and `class_offsets`, the encode-side §8.7.3 SAO parameter search, classified every sample of every CTB into a §8.7.3.2 edge category once per candidate edge-offset class -- four full scalar passes over the picture before the filter itself ran. Both now dispatch through a new `src/hevc/engine/encoder/recon_simd.rs` to SSE4.1 or AVX2 on `x86_64`, NEON on `aarch64`, or the portable scalar reference elsewhere, exactly as `rdcost.rs` already did for the distortion metrics. Reaching them needed the surrounding loops restructured, not just their bodies replaced: the prediction of a row is now gathered contiguously once, by both the exact-residual path the kernel then reconstructs whole and the quantized path that transforms it block by block (a plain copy whenever the motion vector keeps the row inside the reference, falling back to the per-sample §8.5.3.3.2 edge clamp only for a row that hangs off an edge) so the add-and-clip is a straight-line run, and the SAO search's per-sample neighbour bounds test is hoisted into a row range, since for a fixed edge-offset class "both neighbours are inside the plane" is a whole-row condition vertically and a contiguous run horizontally. The public `simd::active_by_site()` gains an entry of its own, `hevc_recon`, so `simd::set_override` is observably reaching these kernels and every benchmark group's override guard covers them. Both kernels are asserted bit-exact against the scalar reference on every instruction set the host supports, at every run length that exercises a full vector body, a partial one and the scalar tail, and the whole reconstruction -- including the SAO decisions it signals -- is asserted identical across every available instruction set for both an intra and an inter picture. Nothing about the encoder's output changes; only how fast a reference picture is reconstructed.
- The AV1 encoder's emitting pass no longer reads a transform-type search back from a size trial's probe. The reuse was added earlier in this same unreleased cycle and is measured out and withdrawn here; what remains of it is the shared reconstruct-and-code path the two searches were made to share, which is worth keeping on its own. What killed it is a ceiling rather than a tuning miss, and `measure_probe_reuse_coverage` is the measurement rather than the argument: on the 640x352 frame at `base_q_idx` 32 the emitting pass writes 1,870 transform blocks and only 44 of them run a transform-type search over more than one candidate at all, because the zero-block shortcut decides 1,716 of them outright and every 32x32 block's §5.11.48 reduced set names a single type. Reading a probe back could therefore never have spared more than those 44 blocks - 220 of the frame's 18,957 candidate evaluations, 1.2% - and at `base_q_idx` 160, where every emitted block is 32x32, the ceiling is zero of 220. The cache reached 21 of the 44. The rest of the gap is where the two key sets diverge, which the same measurement classifies: of the 898 distinct blocks probed at 32, 382 are at a position the winning partition emits at a *different* transform size and 495 at a position that is not the start of an emitted block at all, while not one is a prediction mismatch - a probe lands on the first searched block of every trial of every *candidate* coding block, and the emitting pass walks only the winning partition's blocks. Removing it costs the 105 candidate evaluations it was saving at 32 (0.6%, 18,852 back to 18,957) and none at 160, and no wall-clock change this host can resolve: from the minimum of eight interleaved rounds per arm over three sessions, 0.210 s with the reuse against 0.179 s without at 32 and 0.203 s against 0.182 s at 160 - where the two arms evaluate byte-identically the same 17,044 candidates and produce the same 29,005 bytes, so that 10% spread is the noise floor and not a cost either way. The emitted bitstream is unchanged, `a_fixed_frame_encodes_to_the_same_bytes_on_every_host` passing on its committed digests without regeneration, and `FrameEncoder::probed`, `without_probe_reuse` and the probe's whole-set winner are gone with it; a probe measures what the type search is worth at a size and nothing else, which is what it was for.

- The native HEVC encoder can now be given a target bitrate instead of a fixed quantizer, which is what puts its rate-distortion mode decision into production. `native_hevc_video_encoder_factory`'s backend-private configuration gains a third form beside the existing `[]` (lossless PCM) and `[qp]` (lossy at that `SliceQpY`): four big-endian bytes giving a nonzero target in bits a second, so `1_000_000_u32.to_be_bytes()` asks for a megabit a second. The lengths discriminate, and both existing forms are byte-identical to before, because a caller that names a QP is asking for a picture rather than a rate and a caller that configures nothing should not have its output change. `engine/encoder/ratecontrol.rs` is the new piece: a one-step per-picture QP feedback loop over the standard model that a quantizer step doubles the rate every six QP, so the budget the frame duration implies and the bits the *previous* picture actually cost decide the next picture's `SliceQpY`. The writer is intra-only and every access unit an IDR, so there is no frame-type hierarchy to allocate across and no propagation to model; the loop opens on the bits-per-pixel the budget implies, measured against this writer rather than assumed, and moves at most one full rate doubling a picture so a cut cannot slam the quantizer to a limit. It is all integer arithmetic, including the base-2 logarithm, because a QP that landed differently on a different host would make the bitstream unreproducible. The bitrate path codes through `ModeSearch::RateDistortion` — the decision from the previous release that charges each intra candidate for the residual bits it would code, worth 4 to 9 percent at the same QP — which nothing selected before, because saved bits are only worth saving when there is a rate to spend them under; the variant's dead-code exemption is gone with it. A stream settles within 25 percent of its per-picture budget, which is about as tight as an integer QP picked once a picture can promise, and decodes through `native_hevc_video_decoder_factory` like any other.
- Hardware HEVC decoding now reports what it spends on frame readback separately from the fixed-function decode. Every backend — NVDEC, Windows Media Foundation, and VideoToolbox — maps its own surface and converts it to RGBA inside `submit`, so a caller has only ever seen the two as one number, and for a playback pipeline the host round trip is often the part that bounds throughput. `zvidlib::hevc_hardware_readback` (internal and unstable, alongside `hevc_decoder_bench` and `hevc_decode_profile`) charges each delivered frame to two phases: the surface copy that makes the decoded surface CPU-readable — `cuvidMapVideoFrame` plus `cuMemcpyDtoH`, the staging-texture `CopySubresourceRegion` plus `Map`, or `CVPixelBufferLockBaseAddress` — and the NV12-to-RGBA pass over those bytes. `benches/hevc_hardware.rs` gains a `hevc_hardware_readback` group measuring each phase over the same 32-frame window `steady_state` uses, and prints both as ms/frame and as a share of the decode they are part of. The instrumentation is unconditional, two `Instant::now()` reads and a relaxed `fetch_add` per phase per frame, and its accumulators are process-wide atomics rather than thread-locals because NVDEC and VideoToolbox deliver frames from a callback that need not run on the submitting thread. The decoder's public output is unchanged: a zero-copy surface output path was considered and declined — three platform handle types and an NV12 output family whose contracts the crate does not own — with the reasoning recorded in `benches/README.md`.

- The native HEVC encoder's §8.7.3 SAO search now considers band offset (`SaoTypeIdx == 1`) alongside the four §8.7.3.2 edge-offset classes. `engine/encoder/recon.rs::estimate_sao` evaluates all 32 `sao_band_position` candidates per component — the four consecutive bands each taking their own mean error, clamped to the §7.4.9.3 signalable magnitude and signalled with a `sao_offset_sign` each — and scores them against the edge classes and against leaving SAO off under one `D + lambda * R` comparison, so the type is chosen by what it buys net of what it costs. Band offset's own syntax, the five position bins and its signs, is charged at 2.5x the closed-form multiplier, the coarse end of the departure the slice-level calibration measured, because it is rate that multiplier has never been checked against. The §7.4.9.3 chroma inference still holds: Cb and Cr share one type, each carrying its own offsets and, under band offset, its own `sao_band_position`. Band offset reaches the error edge offset cannot — a CTB biased over a *value range* rather than around a local edge — and the writer's §7.3.8.3 band path, previously never exercised, now reaches the bitstream. Measured over the QP 12–51 sweep on both test pictures against the same writer with SAO off: the noise-carrying picture at 64x48 QP 12 gains an operating point the edge-only search declined outright, +0.100 dB for +0.5% of slice, and at 128x96 five points it already took improve for one to eight bytes more — +0.120 dB at QP 12 against +0.106 before, +0.190 at QP 13, +0.167 at QP 14, +0.118 at QP 17 and +0.127 at QP 19. Every other point of the sweep is byte-identical, no point regresses, and every accepted point still sits on or above the SAO-off writer's own rate-distortion curve. Band offset is never selected on the smooth picture at any QP: that content's error is edge-shaped.
- Equal-cost transform and partition decisions in the AV1 encoder are now settled deliberately instead of by table order. The transform-type search compared candidates with a strict `<`, so a block whose best two types scored an identical `sse + lambda * bits` cost kept whichever one the derived transform set happened to list first - measured at exactly zero margin between `ADST_ADST` and `DCT_DCT` on a 4x4 block of the encoder's own test pattern, with a second block only four units away from a tie between `DCT_DCT` and `IDTX`. That was reproducible, but arbitrary: the winner was a property of a table's order rather than of the block, and any one-unit change to either cost silently flipped it. The type search now ranks on a total order over `(cost, symbol)`, breaking a tie towards the smaller `tx_type` symbol - the one the decoder reads earliest in the set and the cheaper of the two to signal - and the transform-size search breaks its own ties towards the largest transform, whose `read_tx_size` depth symbol is likewise the smallest. The partition search already preferred `PARTITION_NONE` on a tie, the cheaper side to signal, and now says so. Emitted bitstreams are unchanged: both tie-breaks were chosen to be the decision the previous candidate order already produced, so no committed digest moves. What changes is that the encoder no longer has a decision that only holds still because a table has not been reordered - the searches are now asserted to encode identically with their candidates evaluated backwards, and the ties that assertion depends on are asserted to actually occur.
- Re-take the HEVC engine's x86_64 SSE4.1 and AVX2 kernel ratios on an Intel host, so the `hevc::engine::simd` per-kernel table no longer speaks for x86_64 from one vendor's readings. The figures #215 recorded all came from an AMD EPYC-class `ubuntu-latest` runner; the same two `#[ignore]`d stage benchmarks have now been run on an Intel Coffee Lake host (`macos-15-intel`, i7-8700B, `sse4_1` + `avx2`, no AVX-512), three runs of best-of-five-interleaved-rounds each, against a same-day AMD control run so the two are compared on the same code. The question the ticket asked - whether the §8.5.3.3.4 `combine_weighted` SSE4.1 kernel, which measured 0.95x of scalar on Zen and is therefore dispatched to the scalar reference, lands on the other side of 1.00x on an Intel core whose `pmulld` behaves differently - is answered no: with the kernel temporarily restored so it could be timed, it read 1.00x in the block path and 0.75x over an L1-resident buffer on Coffee Lake, further below scalar than on Zen rather than above it. That dispatch is confirmed across both vendors and is unchanged, as is every other dispatch decision: no kernel changes which side of 1.00x it is on between the two. What does differ is magnitude, and the table now carries both vendors side by side rather than one: Intel reads materially lower on everything bounded by vector width (§8.7.3 SAO 2.7-3.0x against 4.6-7.4x, AVX2 `filter_taps` over a buffer 1.7-1.8x against 2.5-2.7x, two-dimensional 8-tap luma 1.6-1.9x against 2.1-2.7x) and slightly higher on §8.7.2 deblocking, which is bounded by edge shape instead. It stays dispatched to unconditionally - the AMD win is large, the Intel cost is a few percent of one kernel, and splitting on a CPUID vendor check would add a second dispatch arm to keep bit-exact for it - and both the module table and the dispatch site now say so rather than leaving the AMD figure to stand for x86_64. No dispatch, kernel or decoded output changes; this is the measurement and what it is recorded against.
- Vectorize the HEVC decoder's 8-bit 4:2:0 YUV-to-RGBA output conversion, which was the largest single stage in a whole-frame decode and the only large one with no vector kernel at all. `picture_to_rgba` applied the fixed BT.601/709 limited-range integer matrix one sample at a time, and the issue #189 stage attribution measured it at a third of everything the whole-frame benchmark groups time - on the path of both `hevc_decode_1080p` and `hevc_decode/<isa>`, so every whole-frame SIMD number the suite reported was diluted by roughly a third for a stage no HEVC kernel touched. Every backend is bit-exact with that reference - the arithmetic is `i32` throughout in the same order with the same arithmetic `>> 16`, a decoded 8-bit picture's samples are clipped to `0..=255` so no product can reach the scalar path's saturation, and a new test checks all of it across widths that leave a tail for both vector widths and across both chroma column parities. - `AacSampleReader::get_range` no longer throws away its decoded-packet cache every time a read advances. `ensure_decoded` treated `decoded` as all-or-nothing: any packet missing from the requested window reset the decoder, cleared the map, and re-decoded the window plus `preroll_packets`. Forward playback, which shares almost every packet with the read before it, therefore cost the same per request as a cold seek - about 100 us against about 1.5 us for a range already resident. The reader now tracks the contiguous run of packets the decoder has produced and, when a request starts inside that run or immediately after it, decodes only the access units past its end with no reset and no preroll; cold, backwards, and gapped requests keep the reset-plus-preroll path a real seek needs. Retained packets are bounded by the `Limits` knobs that already govern a decode - at most `max_decode_samples_per_seek` packets holding at most `max_allocation_bytes` of samples - with the packets furthest behind the request evicted first, and the per-read copy now visits only the buffers that overlap the request rather than the whole cache. Decoded output is unchanged.

- The native HEVC encoder's lossy residual writer now runs the ITU-T H.265 §8.7.3 sample adaptive offset filter behind the §8.7.2 deblocking pass, in the §8.7.1 order. `engine/encoder/lossy.rs` emits `sample_adaptive_offset_enabled_flag == 1` in the SPS and the `slice_sao_luma_flag` / `slice_sao_chroma_flag` pair in the slice header, and codes the §7.3.8.3 `sao( )` structure per CTB — merge flags, `sao_type_idx`, `sao_offset_abs`, `sao_offset_sign`, `sao_band_position` and `sao_eo_class` — with the §9.3.3 binarizations and §9.3.4.2 contexts the decoder already parses. The parameters come from `engine/encoder/recon.rs::estimate_sao`, which was already there and unused by this writer; it now decides the two chroma components together on one shared class, because §7.4.9.3 infers `SaoTypeIdx[2]` and `SaoEoClass[2]` from cIdx 1 and a Cb and a Cr that picked different classes are not a bitstream. What `write_idr_residual_slice` returns is therefore still exactly what a conforming decoder reconstructs, sample for sample, at every QP, on both the smooth and the noise-carrying test picture.

  SAO is on, and the writer turns it off per slice when it does not pay. Unlike deblocking, which is signalled once in the PPS, SAO costs a `sao( )` structure on every CTB including every CTB it does nothing to, so the decision is taken twice. Per CTB a class is signalled only when the squared error it removes clears the bins it would be coded with, against the same closed-form `rdo::lambda_q8` the mode search uses — inside a picture already committed to the syntax, that is a choice between two codings of one CTB at one QP, which is the trade that multiplier is derived for. Per slice the whole pass is kept only when the error it actually removed clears the bits the whole grid actually costs, and that decision is taken against a multiplier measured off this picture's own rate-distortion curve rather than the closed form: the choice there is between spending bits on SAO and spending the same bits on a finer quantizer, so what the bits are worth is a property of the content, and the closed form misses this picture's own slope by 0.4x to 2.5x over the sweep. `curve_point` codes the same picture one QP finer, `calibrated_sao_lambda_q8` reads off the curve through those two points what the grid's bits are worth, and `keeps_sao` takes that probe only where its answer can still change the decision — outside the band the calibrated multiplier is clamped to, the closed form settles it alone, which over the sweep is half the pictures. When the pass is declined the reconstruction reverts to the deblocked one and both slice flags go out as 0, which §7.3.8.3 reads as "code nothing" and leaves the cost at the two header bits.

  Measured against the same writer with SAO off, at the same QP with the same mode decisions, in whole-picture PSNR and slice bytes over a QP 12 to 51 sweep on both test pictures at 64x48 and 128x96: the pass is taken at 36 of those points and buys +0.07 to +0.89 dB for +0.4% to +13.4% of the slice, including +0.73 dB for +11.4% on smooth content at QP 12 at 64x48 and +0.89 dB for +13.4% at 128x96. At every other point of the sweep the slice-level test declines it and the slice is the deblocked writer's to the byte, or within one byte of it. Every accepted point sits on or above the SAO-off writer's own rate-distortion curve, interpolated in log-rate at the same slice size — from +0.001 dB at the closest to +0.55 dB at the two clear wins. That is what the fixed multiplier could not do: it declined 30 of these points and accepted two others, the noise picture at QP 32, that sat 0.08 and 0.14 dB below that curve. The measurement costs the writer a second decision pass on the pictures where it is taken: the whole SAO stage is 19% to 25% of a picture's encode time where the band settles the decision without a probe, and 127% to 148% where it does not.

  The coding loop is now two passes over the picture rather than one, because §7.3.8.3 puts a CTB's SAO parameters at the head of that CTB's slice data while §8.7.1 only lets them be searched once the whole picture is coded and deblocked: a decision pass searches every coding unit and builds the reconstruction, the filters run, and a bitstream pass codes the recorded decisions with the `sao( )` syntax in front of each. Nothing a decision depends on changes in between — §8.4.4.2.2 intra prediction reads the unfiltered reconstruction, which the decision pass has already built — so the picture that is coded is exactly the picture that was searched, and no coding unit is searched twice.

The 2-D `IDTX` type runs that same scaled identity on both axes and takes the same `transform_shift` as every other type, so it reconstructs at the magnitude a conforming decoder produces rather than at the raw dequantized one, and it dispatches to the vector kernels like the rest instead of taking a short path around them; only the ADST passes still fall back to the DCT above `TX_16X16`, since the identity is defined at every size. The encoder-side `forward_transform` applies the same scale, so a round trip through a half-identity type cancels exactly as it does for the others, and the SSE4.1/AVX2/NEON kernels cover the new types bit-exactly against the scalar path rather than falling back to it. New coverage decodes a bitstream signalling each new type - `V_DCT`/`H_DCT` from `TX_SET_INTRA_1` and `TX_SET_INTER_2`, and all sixteen `TX_SET_INTER_1` entries from an 8x8 inter transform block under `TX_MODE_SELECT` - and checks each reconstructs bit-exactly through the matching kernel and distinguishably from every other type. Decoded output for the checked-in AV1 elementary streams is unchanged: they signal no `tx_type` symbol.

- State what the HEVC writer's slice-level SAO acceptance rule is actually deciding, and measure how well it can decide it. #287 read the rule as not monotone in the grid it judges: `keeps_sao` charges a candidate grid at `calibrated_sao_lambda_q8(fine, coarse, qp, sao_bits)`, a multiplier that is itself a function of the rate being judged, and because the picture's own curve is convex a smaller grid is charged a higher price per bit - so at noise 64x48 QP 12 a full grid gaining 75 for 104 bits was declined at a multiplier of 190 while the subset grid gaining 54 for 72 bits was accepted. It is not non-monotone, and stating the rule in the units it decides in is what shows that. The decision is not `D + lambda * R` against a price at all: it is a comparison of two rate-distortion points at the same rate - the squared error SAO removed, against the squared error the same bits would remove spent on a finer quantizer, read off this picture's own curve - and the new `sao_threshold_sse` is that quantity, in the picture SSE units the gain is already measured in. In that form the rule is monotone by construction and now asserted to be, by `the_slice_level_threshold_is_monotone_in_the_grid_it_judges`, over every probe shape the calibration accepts and across both clamps of the trust band. The two decisions #287 read as contradictory are consistent: the extra 32 bits raised the threshold by 23 and bought 21, so the extra components paid against the per-CTB search's constant multiplier but not against the curve, which is a different instrument measuring a different trade. What a superset grid has to beat is the curve's marginal distortion at the rate being judged and not its average out to that rate, and the multiplier is that average, which is why dividing an increasing concave threshold by the rate produced a falling price and read as a defect. `keeps_sao` decides on the threshold directly rather than routing through `round(reachable * 256 / sao_bits)` and back, so the Q8 per-bit round-trip - the one thing in the old form that genuinely was not monotone, its rounding direction flipping with the rate - is gone.

  How precisely it decides is the other half, and it is now measured rather than assumed. The rule compares one quantity it measures exactly against one it extrapolates from a two-point probe, and only the second can be wrong. `measure_sao_acceptance_precision` quantifies it against the same ladder `sao_curve_offsets` interpolates - this writer coded at every QP from 0 to 51 - by holding one rung out and predicting it from its neighbours, which is the model doing its own job with a known answer, and an easier job than the shipped one on both counts, a chord over two quantizer steps read one step from its anchor where the writer's is over one step read a fraction of one. It is wrong by tens of percent of the distortion it predicts: worst 50.5% on noise 128x96, 196% on noise 64x48 where a quantizer step is only tens of bits wide, 0.006 dB to 0.34 dB in the currency the invariant is stated in. `SAO_ACCEPTANCE_RESOLUTION_NUM` records that at 60%, and `the_acceptance_model_cannot_resolve_the_margins_it_decides_on` holds it from both sides so a model that quietly became sharp would fail rather than pass. What the number settles is that no tolerance around the comparison helps, which was the obvious repair and is the wrong one: the model's resolution at noise 128x96 QP 32 is 0.13 dB, forty times the 0.003 dB `every_accepted_sao_point_sits_on_the_writers_own_curve` measures that slice below the curve, and also larger than the 0.100 dB win the same rule buys at noise 64x48 QP 12 - measured, by trying it, and a margin wide enough to refuse the failure refuses the win. Every operating point this writer has, win and failure alike, sits inside the acceptance model's own resolution.

  So the fixed 2.5x band-syntax charge in `SaoLambda::band_q8` stays, and now for a measured reason rather than for want of a replacement. The QP 12-51 sweep was re-taken on both test pictures at both sizes at 1.4x and 1.1x, the two ends of what the probe measures a marginal bit at, against the repaired rule and against the one before it: all four runs agree to the byte, the same noise 128x96 QP 32 slice landing 2079 bytes and -0.003 dB off its own curve, so the floor of the [1.5x, 4x) window is not something the rule's restatement moves. At 1.5x the invariant holds and the QPs carrying band components go from 13 to 27, which is the whole of what lowering the charge would buy; it is refused, because a constant sitting on the exact boundary where the invariant is next measured to break is fitted to that boundary rather than derived from anything. What the charge stands in for is the resolution above - the invariant measures a gap the acceptance rule cannot see, so what closes it has to be a price on the search rather than a tolerance on the rule. No SAO decision, reconstructed picture or bitstream changes: the whole sweep is byte-identical at the shipped charge.

- The HEVC decoder's §8.7.3 sample-adaptive-offset filter now reaches its vector kernels, which it never did. Issue #189's breakdown put `sao` at 14.7% of decode proper on the `neon` arm and marked it vectorized, and issue #310 asked why its two arms sat at 0.99x while the isolated `hevc_sao` group measured 1.57x. The answer was not a slow kernel or a diluted stage: no in-decode SAO sample reached a kernel at all. §8.7.3.2's classification has to deny a neighbour read crossing a slice or tile boundary with filtering across it disabled, and the branch-free row path that calls `sao_edge_row` / `sao_band_row` was taken only when the caller passed no boundary grids whatsoever — which a decoder cannot do, since whether a stream is single-slice and single-tile is not known before it is parsed. The same held for the §8.7.3.1 PCM / transquant-bypass suppression map, present for a whole picture as soon as one coding unit anywhere in it qualifies. Both tests are now asked about the coding tree block instead: a CTB whose eight neighbours are all mutually filterable has no sample the per-sample test could deny, band offset reads no neighbour at all, and a CTB that fails either test still takes the scalar path, which stays the normative reference. Measured on 48 frames of the bundled 1920x1080 sample on Apple Silicon, `--release`, minimum of six interleaved rounds per arm with the two builds run alternately: the stage falls from 3.10 to 0.90 ms/frame on the `neon` arm (3.4x) and 3.07 to 1.23 on `scalar` (2.5x), taking a whole frame from 23.46 to 21.95 ms on `neon` and 32.54 to 30.45 on `scalar` — 6.4% on both. The arithmetic is the same `i32` operations in the same order, so decoded output is unchanged bit-for-bit; `tests/codec_conformance.rs` passes on its committed per-frame SHA-256 digests without regeneration. The stage is also three attribution rows rather than one now (`sao_filter`, `sao_snapshot`, `sao_setup`), and the `hevc_sao` benchmark runs the per-CTB parameter mix a real decode signals — 86.7% of luma and 94.4% of chroma CTBs take no SAO at all — instead of a grid that filtered every CTB of every component; `benches/README.md` records both.
